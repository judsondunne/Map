What you are looking at

Every basemap in this tool is a real, georeferenced historical map or aerial photograph of Philadelphia. Nothing is illustrated or invented. The 3D massing is built from the City of Philadelphia's own LiDAR-derived building footprints, dated by the Office of Property Assessment's construction-year records.

3D buildings

  • Geometry & height — City of Philadelphia LI_BUILDING_FOOTPRINTS: 66,698 footprints in the historic core, with LiDAR roof heights (approx_hgt) in feet above grade.
  • Construction year — Office of Property Assessment opa_properties_public. 94.0% of footprints are dated by a direct street-address match, 3.9% by the median of their parcel's records, and 2.1% estimated from neighbouring buildings.

Honest caveats

  • Pre-1682 is a reconstruction. No survey map of the site exists before Thomas Holme's. That era shows real terrain and real river geometry with a woodland treatment — it is an impression, not a document.
  • Assessor rounding. OPA construction years cluster heavily on 1920 and 1925 for older rowhouses, because that was the assessor's default for "old". The 1910s–20s therefore look like a sharper building boom than it was. It remains the only citywide dated dataset that exists.
  • Survivor bias. These are buildings standing today, dated by when they were built. Demolished buildings are not in the dataset, so earlier years show a thinner city than really stood there. The historic basemaps underneath show what has been lost.
  • Big buildings are dated worst. OPA is reliable for the row-house fabric — the overwhelming majority of these footprints — but for large commercial buildings year_built often records a renovation, a company's founding year, or simply the oldest record on a shared lot. City Hall is listed as 2015. A 173 m tower at 1911 Walnut carries an 1800 record.
  • Two impossible-date checks are applied. Nothing in Philadelphia stood above about 40 m before the steel-frame 1890s, and by long convention nothing exceeded City Hall's 167 m until One Liberty Place broke it in 1987. Eight footprints violate one of those and have their date discarded and re-estimated. "Tallest structure" only ever counts buildings dated directly from a record.
  • Heights are roofs, not spires. approx_hgt is used rather than the raw LiDAR maximum, which borrows height from whatever overhangs a footprint — it reads the Shops at Liberty Place as 700 ft instead of 56 ft. The trade is that spires are cut: Comcast Center measures 297 m here, correct to its roof, while the Technology Center's mast is not counted.
